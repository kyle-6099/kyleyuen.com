How many times have you heard someone say, "This is how we've done it since forever"?

Most roles are not designed. They are inherited. A process that made sense ten years ago gets patched, outsourced, partially automated, and handed to the next hire. By now it is a bundle of rituals nobody questions.

When leaders see this, their first instinct is to buy automation. They want the tool to do the ritual faster. But automation without redesign just speeds up bad work. It embeds the old logic into software, where it becomes even harder to change.

The better move is to treat the role like something that has been rebuilt in layers. Before you automate anything, you refactor:

  • What is the actual outcome this role exists to produce?
  • Which steps require human judgment?
  • Which steps are just carrying information from one place to another?
  • What would the workflow look like if it were designed today?

Only after that should you decide whether the fix is automation, AI, a system change, or a redefined role. Sometimes the right answer is not a faster tool — it is removing a step entirely.

AI is forcing this question on every organization. The companies that get ahead will be the ones that redesign before they automate.
